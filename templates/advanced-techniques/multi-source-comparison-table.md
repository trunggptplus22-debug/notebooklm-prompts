# Method, Model, and Evidence Comparison Matrix

## Objective
Compare competing methods, theories, algorithms, tools, or sources on a common analytical basis.

## Inputs
- Question / decision: [INSERT]
- Items to compare: [INSERT OR DISCOVER]
- Sources / data: [INSERT OR NONE]
- Constraints and priorities: [INSERT]

## Method
Normalize terminology and define the comparison dimensions before scoring. Compare each item on: foundational assumptions; represented entities and variables; mechanism; required inputs; outputs; formal rules; computational feasibility; evidence quality; known limitations; edge-case behavior; extensibility; implementation cost.

Distinguish direct evidence from interpretation. Do not assign numerical scores without defined scales and evidence. Where information is missing, mark it unknown rather than infer equivalence.

## Output
1. Comparison matrix.
2. Material similarities and differences.
3. Hidden incompatibilities and translation costs.
4. Conditions under which each option is preferable.
5. Best-fit recommendation with explicit decision criteria.
6. Missing evidence and targeted research questions.
7. A falsification test for the leading option.
