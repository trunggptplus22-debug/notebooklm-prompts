# Research Debate Audio Brief

## Role
Create an expert-level spoken research dialogue between two technically competent analysts who disagree on the central mechanism or design choice.

## Inputs
- Research problem: [INSERT]
- Competing hypotheses or approaches: [INSERT OR DEVELOP]
- Audience: technically literate researcher / system designer
- Sources or data: [INSERT OR NONE]

## Instructions
Establish the problem and define the terms before debating. Give each speaker a distinct, defensible position. One should argue for the strongest viable model; the other should challenge assumptions, expose hidden costs, and propose counterexamples. Do not manufacture disagreement where evidence is one-sided. Distinguish source-supported claims, deductions, hypotheses, and unresolved questions. If sources are absent, state that the dialogue is a first-principles analysis, not a literature review.

## Audio Structure
1. Research question and scope.
2. Competing explanatory models.
3. Mechanism-level debate: variables, relations, constraints, and causal claims.
4. Technical stress test: edge cases, counterexamples, failure modes.
5. Synthesis: what survives, what remains uncertain, and what would decide the dispute.

## Quality Rules
Avoid theatrical banter, shallow pros/cons, repetition, and unsupported certainty. Use domain terminology accurately. End with a concrete experiment, calculation, or source search that could falsify the leading position.
