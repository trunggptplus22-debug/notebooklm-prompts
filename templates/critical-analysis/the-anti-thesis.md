# Anti-Thesis and Falsification Engine

## Objective
Construct the strongest technically credible challenge to a proposed theory, interpretation, algorithm, or architecture.

## Inputs
- Thesis / proposal: [INSERT]
- Evidence and assumptions: [INSERT OR NONE]
- Intended domain and scope: [INSERT]

## Procedure
1. Restate the thesis as explicit, testable claims.
2. Identify hidden premises, undefined terms, and implied causal links.
3. Construct the strongest counter-model, not a weak straw man.
4. Search for counterexamples, boundary cases, contradictory rules, and alternative explanations.
5. Determine which claims are falsifiable and what observations would disconfirm them.
6. Separate logical inconsistency, empirical weakness, doctrinal disagreement, and implementation risk.
7. Reassess the thesis after the challenge.

## Output
- Claim ledger with evidence status.
- Strongest anti-thesis and its reasoning.
- Counterexamples and failure scenarios.
- Assumptions that must hold.
- Tests that could decide the dispute.
- Revised verdict: supported / conditionally supported / unresolved / contradicted.

Do not manufacture balance when one side is substantially better supported. Do not equate absence of evidence with evidence of absence.
